## Degraded-mode / autonomy-reversion hypothesis

If a manufacturer combines approved degraded/manual operating modes, OT/automation dependency, shift-specific competence and operational value-at-risk, then it can identify and close human fallback gaps more effectively than a conventional rarity-only skills matrix.

Simple alternative: train the globally rarest skill first.

Success threshold: risk-weighted approach must beat rarity-only in at least 60% of non-trivial synthetic scenarios, reduce median modelled workforce fallback exposure by at least 25%, and make zero recommendations to manually operate an asset where degraded/manual operation is not pre-approved.

Stop criterion: stop or redesign if realistic replay cannot beat rarity-only by 15% median, if required OT-to-competence mapping is not available or trustworthy, or if the design pressures users to bypass engineering/safety controls.

Safety boundary: the engine can diagnose staffing/training readiness only. It cannot command OT, change machine state, bypass interlocks, approve degraded/manual operation, or convert an unapproved fallback into a workforce action.

Scale inversion: a 1-person site should receive an explicit "no internal fallback" diagnosis and engineering/external-support options rather than a false training recommendation; large multi-site deployments remain site-scoped and never borrow competence silently across sites.
