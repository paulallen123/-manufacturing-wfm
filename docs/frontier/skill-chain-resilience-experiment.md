# Frontier experiment — Skill-Chain Resilience

## Discovery ownership
Frontier & Cross-Domain Innovation owns discovery. Product Evolution owns any eventual production UX/optimisation. Integration & Data Fabric owns MES/ERP/BOM/supplier/skills semantics. Trust/Proof/Release owns reliability, security and claim evidence.

## New connection
Connect supply-chain/process criticality to workforce skill redundancy. The question is not only “do we have enough people?” or “is this supplier/component critical?” but “which product/flow becomes fragile because too few currently qualified people can execute the process needed when a supply or production disruption occurs?”

## Falsifiable hypothesis
A value-aware skill dependency stress test can identify internal cross-training actions that reduce stressed production/value exposure more than a simple alternative of training the rarest skill first, without asserting competence or disruption probabilities.

## Simple alternative
Rank skills only by number of qualified workers and train the rarest first.

## Success threshold
Continue only if, on representative synthetic and replayable customer-like scenarios, the value-aware ranking reduces stressed value/throughput exposure by at least 20% versus baseline and materially beats rarity-only prioritisation in at least 80% of multi-site scenarios where both have eligible options. Zero cross-site, cross-tenant or expired-certification competence leakage is allowed.

## Stop criterion
Reject or redesign if required process-to-skill mappings cannot be sourced reliably, if the value-aware ranking fails to outperform rarity-only training materially, or if recommendations depend on invented disruption probabilities. If no eligible training pathway exists, fail honestly to external backup/process redesign/automation rather than inventing a worker option.

## Safety boundary
Advisory and simulation only. It cannot certify a worker, assign a worker, change a schedule, weaken a qualification rule or infer protected/sensitive characteristics. Training eligibility and competence remain authoritative in WFM/LMS/HR systems. Supply criticality must come from an authoritative process/supply source.

## Commercial value
Potential value includes fewer production stoppages, more targeted training spend, less emergency overtime/agency cover, faster disruption recovery and stronger evidence for continuity planning. Value is not claimed until replay or real operational outcomes support it.

## Scale inversion
- 1 employee / 1 site: the engine must not invent cross-training. It should expose a single-person dependency and recommend an external backup, approved training pathway, process redesign or automation option.
- 10,000+ employees / many sites: skill coverage remains site/process scoped. Skills at another site cannot silently satisfy local competence. Cross-site transfer is a separate governed option requiring travel, legal, language, union/policy and certification checks.

## Global/future relevance
Useful where localisation, nearshoring, supply-chain diversification, robotics and ageing workforces change which capabilities are strategically scarce. Particularly relevant to aerospace/defence, semiconductors, pharmaceuticals/biomanufacturing, contract manufacturing and specialist process industries, but the primitive remains generic.

## Evidence position — 20 September 2026
- NIST supply-chain guidance treats delayed materials as a cause of work stoppage/missed shipment and recommends mapping/risk assessment.
- NIST’s Manufacturing USA workforce analysis published 2 June 2026 maps 132 advanced-manufacturing occupations to 235 knowledge/skills/abilities across multiple technology domains.
- WEF’s Human-Machine Collaboration Framework published 23 June 2026 says three in four industrial jobs are expected to evolve and around 40% of future industrial skills are new/emerging.
- SAP’s Hannover Messe 2026 material publicly connects workforce scheduling to operational demand and separately describes supplier-reliability/workforce agents. Public material checked did not show the narrower decision primitive implemented here: prioritising cross-training from process/BOM/supply-chain value-at-risk under a deterministic skill-loss stress test. That is a comparison of public positioning, not proof no competitor has similar internal capability.

## Current implementation
A bounded deterministic engine now:
- requires authoritative process map, skill/certification and supply-criticality evidence with provenance;
- stress-tests loss of qualified capacity without inventing a disruption probability;
- identifies exposed flows and value-at-risk;
- evaluates only explicitly eligible training candidates;
- ranks candidates by exposure reduction, with training-hours/cost available for later optimisation;
- provides an honest backup-required fallback for micro sites with no internal candidate;
- keeps global sites isolated so another site’s skill does not count locally.

## Self-learning path
If eventually trialled: recommendation -> accepted/rejected action -> training completion -> qualification evidence -> disruption/absence event -> operational outcome -> financial outcome -> confidence update. Predicted, simulated and realised outcomes remain distinct. Learning may calibrate prioritisation evidence but cannot auto-certify or override deterministic controls.
